Presets

XY oscilloscope

Left channel (X) and right channel (Y). Equal frequencies give an ellipse or a line; whole-number ratios (e.g. 1:2, 2:3) give stable Lissajous figures.

Start with a low volume. Square and sawtooth waves sound louder than a sine at the same setting. High tones can be unpleasant and harmful to your hearing.

Free online tone generator: 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz

This online frequency generator plays pure sine, square, sawtooth and triangle waves across the whole audible range, from 20 Hz to 20 kHz. Every stereo channel has its own frequency, waveform and volume, so you can test the left and right side of headphones or speakers independently. Precision can be set to 1, 0.1 or 0.01 Hz.

What you can use it for

  • Left/right channel test for headphones and speakers
  • Checking frequency response and finding rattles or resonances in a device or room
  • Binaural-beat style experiments: set two close tones, for example 440 Hz and 442 Hz
  • Tuning an instrument against a reference pitch such as A4 = 440 Hz
  • Learning how waveform shape changes timbre and harmonics
  • Drawing Lissajous figures on the XY oscilloscope

Waveforms explained

Sine wave
A pure tone with a single frequency and no harmonics.
Square wave
Contains odd harmonics, so it sounds hollow and buzzy.
Sawtooth wave
Contains all harmonics, so it sounds bright and sharp.
Triangle wave
Odd harmonics that fade quickly, giving a softer sound than a square wave.

Listening safely

Start at a low volume and avoid listening to loud or very high tones for long periods.
